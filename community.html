A note from our founder

Caring for families navigating a new diagnosis

For decades, medical literature characterized cystic fibrosis primarily as a Caucasian disease, meaning local doctors simply didn't look out for it when investigating patient symptoms. That narrative changed 13 years ago with the first confirmed diagnosis of a Ghanaian child, followed 7 years later by a second, proving definitively that CF is present within our population.

Today, those two pioneers are 13 and 7 years old. They are thriving not by chance, but through the relentless dedication of medical professionals, supportive employers, and friends and family who cared enough to make a difference. Yet, in spite of all the support and progress over the years, the global standard for establishing a diagnosis of cystic fibrosis in Ghana still does not exist. Beyond diagnosis, standard care is not only extraordinarily expensive, but it also requires a coordinated multidisciplinary team of specialists working in concert to manage this lifelong condition.

Why the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation Ghana exists

  • Heighten Awareness & Medical Literacy. Bring cystic fibrosis to the forefront of clinical education so doctors actively recognize and search for CF when investigating symptoms.
  • Improve Diagnostic Capacity. Join forces across the healthcare ecosystem to expand diagnostic capacity and establish standardized testing protocols locally.
  • Bridge Treatment Gaps. Transition from ad-hoc support to a sustainable, national infrastructure for complex, multi-systemic care.
  • Global Collaboration. Build strategic international partnerships to access cutting-edge therapies, specialized expertise, and essential funding.
  • Advocate for Patient Care. Ensure that children in Ghana living with CF receive the visibility, care, and long-term support they deserve.

The Foundation exists to scale up the progress made with the two pioneering children into systemic care, ensuring every Ghanaian child living with CF has an advocate, a community, and a chance at a healthy life.

A note from our researcher

Dr. Lily Paemka, Human Molecular Geneticist

As a human molecular geneticist, my motivation for being part of CF Foundation Ghana is deeply rooted in both my previous experience in cystic fibrosis research and my broader interest in understanding genetic variation in African populations. During my postdoctoral training in the United States, I worked in cystic fibrosis research at a time when CF had not yet been identified or recognised in Ghana.

My current research interests include studying novel genetic variation in African populations, which remain significantly underrepresented in global genomic research. I am particularly interested in understanding how population-specific genetic variation may influence disease presentation, diagnosis, and ultimately patient care.

Being part of CF Foundation Ghana provides an opportunity to bring these interests together, supporting greater awareness and advocacy while also contributing to efforts to better understand the genetic basis of CF in Ghanaian and African populations. I am motivated by the potential to help generate knowledge that is relevant to our own populations and translate this into improved diagnosis, research, and care for individuals and families affected by CF.
